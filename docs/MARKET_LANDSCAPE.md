# Market landscape: film and TV discovery, friend recommendations, and mood

Research date: 2026-11-13. Method: desk research from public web pages, product listings, press and a few academic papers. This is not a user survey, a market-size estimate, or hands-on testing of every app. Feature claims come from each product's own pages or press coverage and were not confirmed inside the apps. "Unverified" means I could not find a source. "Not found" means I looked and found no evidence, which is weaker than proof of absence.

Earlier work is not repeated here: Beli interaction research is in [BELI_RESEARCH.md](BELI_RESEARCH.md); stack and API research is in [ADR 0001](adr/0001-platform-data-and-ai-boundaries.md).

## The short version

- The "remember who recommended it" idea is no longer empty space. At least eight small apps now do some version of it. I found no evidence of scale for any of them.
- The large players are moving toward social and feeling-based features: Plex announced emoji "how it made you feel" reactions and a Match Score, Netflix launched a mood-theme AI picker, Spotify brought back friend messaging.
- Nobody found connects all four steps: a recommendation with a reason, the later viewing, a structured non-numeric response, and a reply to the recommender.
- Three large structural facts changed in 2026: TV Time shut down, the New York Times is in advanced talks to buy Letterboxd, and TMDB staff confirmed that any app revenue requires a commercial plan.

## What changed in 2025 and 2026

**Letterboxd.** It reported 30.7 million members at 30 June 2026, up 43 percent in a year ([source](https://bebeez.eu/2026/11/12/new-york-times-in-exclusive-acquisition-talks-with-tiny-fund-for-330-million-us-dollars-acquisition-of-letterboxd/)). More than half of members are under 35 ([source](https://time.com/collection/time100-most-influential-companies/2026/letterboxd/)). The New York Times Company entered exclusive talks on 5 November 2026 to buy it for about 330 million USD; no deal was final at the time of writing ([source](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/05/business/media/letterboxd-new-york-times-company.html), [source](https://deadline.com/2026/11/new-york-times-company-exclusive-talks-letterboxd-1237215118/)). It launched Video Store rentals in December 2025, curated as "shelves" ([source](https://letterboxd.com/journal/letterboxd-video-store/)). TV seasons were promised for 2026 in a separate area of the app ([source](https://time.com/collection/time100-most-influential-companies/2026/letterboxd/)); the help page still says returning series are not supported ([source](https://support.letterboxd.com/hc/en-us/articles/15269096507407-Do-you-support-TV-shows)). Letterboxd says it has no section labelled recommendations and sees the whole site as "one big, organic recommendation engine" ([source](https://support.letterboxd.com/hc/en-us/articles/15178828078223-Can-Letterboxd-generate-recommendations-for-me)). Private lists can be shared with friends by secret link since 2023, and the company said collaboration is "on the horizon" ([source](https://letterboxd.com/journal/between-us-private-lists-sharing/)). Diary entries now offer a "Close Friends" privacy mode ([source](https://letterboxd.com/about/faq/)); launch date unverified. I found no send-to-a-friend feature.

**Letterboxd API.** Access is by request only and excludes recommendation projects, LLM use, and personal projects ([source](https://letterboxd.com/api-beta/)). Competitors work around this by importing the user's own Letterboxd export file ([LaterRex](https://laterrex.com/blog/public-beta-live), [Ranked](https://rankedmovies.app/guides/beli-for-movies/), [Telli](https://watchtelli.com/compare/letterboxd-for-tv-shows)). Import of an export file is now table stakes.

**TV Time closed.** It shut down on 15 July 2026 with over 25 million registered users. The stated reason: it was "no longer sustainable" as a free app and "there was not enough demand for a paid app" ([source](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/02/popular-tv-tracking-app-tv-time-is-shutting-down-as-company-focuses-on-ai/)).

**Trakt.** VIP doubled to 60 USD a year in 2025 and free accounts were capped ([source](https://www.neowin.net/news/trakt-vip-receives-up-to-300-price-hike-going-back-on-promise-to-honor-legacy-subs/), [source](https://alternativeto.net/news/2025/2/trakt-tv-has-set-stricter-limits-for-free-users-and-raised-vip-subscription-prices-by-100-/)). One long-time user reports that creating API apps was restricted to VIP members in July 2026 ([source](https://laim.scot/blog/enshittification-of-trakt-tv)); this is a single blog, not an official statement.

**Plex.** It claims over 42 million monthly active users ([source](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/03/plex-launches-new-social-features-amid-a-major-price-hike/)). In June 2026 it announced lists, public discussions, a Match Score, and emoji reactions "that capture how a title made them feel", rolling out through the year ([source](https://www.plex.tv/blog/plex-introduces-a-social-platform-for-entertainment-discovery-across-streaming-services/)).

**Likewise.** New owners rebranded the company as Pix Media in October 2025 and centred it on newsletters ([source](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/josh-epstein-13b961_goodbye-likewiseits-been-a-good-runbut-activity-7383909044547379200-krZX), [source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Likewise,_Inc.)). A third-party tracker says the iPhone app left the App Store in August 2026 ([source](https://www.appbrain.com/appstore/likewise-movie-tv-book-recs/ios-1264195462)), while the Android listing was updated in September 2026 ([source](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?hl=en_US&id=com.Likewise.apps.Likewise)). Status is unverified.

**Adjacent media.** Fable (books, plus TV) removed its AI reader summaries in January 2025 after they produced offensive text ([source](https://www.wired.com/story/fable-controversy-ai-summaries/)), then was acquired by Scribd's Everand in June 2025 ([source](https://www.scribdinc.com/newsroom/fable-joins-everand)). Musicboard had long outages from late 2025, went quiet, and never offered a data export ([source](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/09/so-whats-going-on-with-musicboard/)). Backloggd, funded through Patreon, started its first mobile app beta on 13 November 2026 ([source](https://insider-gaming.com/backloggd-gamings-version-of-letterboxd-gets-first-mobile-app-closed-beta/)).

**IMDb.** In November 2026 it brought member profiles and a Block feature to its apps ([source](https://community-imdb.sprinklr.com/conversations/imdbcom/profiles-on-apps-block-and-more/6af365bb4e74410ab8135bfc)).

## Friend-recommendation apps: alive, dead, and why

Alive and small. LaterRex left beta in July 2026; it is built by one developer ([source](https://laterrex.com/blog/laterrex-is-live), [source](https://www.hunted.space/product/laterrex)). Rexx shows 33 App Store ratings ([source](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/rexx-save-share-ideas/id6751042420)). Shortlist shows 20 ([source](https://shortlist.watch/)). I found no user counts for any app in this group.

Dead or retreated, with stated reasons:

- **Netflix Friends (2004 to 2010).** It let members suggest films to each other and see friends' ratings. Netflix removed it because about 2 percent of subscribers used it ([source](https://betanews.com/article/netflix-axes-friends-feature-due-to-unpopularity/)). A former Netflix product leader says connection reached 8 percent after four years, and the company judged it needed at least 20 percent to move retention ([source](https://www.mindtheproduct.com/why-we-fail-what-i-learned-from-5-years-with-friends-netflixs-social-strategy/)).
- **Spotify inbox (removed 2017).** Spotify cited "very low engagement". It relaunched Messages in August 2025, seeded with people the user had already shared with ([source](https://musically.com/2025/08/27/spotify-brings-messaging-back-eight-years-after-removing-it/)).
- **Sofa shared lists.** The beta ended in 2024; the developer said he needed to "rethink how sharing will work" ([source](https://9to5mac.com/2024/04/30/award-winning-sofa-downtime-organizer-major-update/)).
- **Queue.** It launched in 2021 on "real recommendations from real friends" ([source](https://dot.la/queue-app-2656055443.html)) and claimed over one million downloads in a year by 2023 ([source](https://variety.com/2023/film/news/queue-app-todd-lieberman-david-gordon-green-hunger-games-ballad-of-songbirds-amp-snakes-promotion-1235763052/)). Its site now presents as a where-to-watch guide ([source](https://www.queue.co/)). Current app status is unverified.

A detail worth noting: LaterRex added Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Reddit and others as recommendation sources in August 2026 because "a lot of what you save comes from a reel or a subreddit rather than a person" ([source](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/laterrex-movie-tv-backlog/id6757280650)).

## Beli: mechanics, growth, and what transferred

Mechanics. A user picks liked, fine, or did not like, then answers comparisons against places already ranked; the app derives a 1 to 10 score ([source](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/08/04/lifestyle/beli-restaurant-rating-app/)). It shows a predicted "rec score" and a friend score ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beli_(app))). Feeds show only people the user follows ([source](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/08/04/lifestyle/beli-restaurant-rating-app/)). It has city and global leaderboards and streaks ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beli_(app))).

Growth. More than 120 million ratings by August 2026, with a team of about ten ([source](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/08/04/lifestyle/beli-restaurant-rating-app/)). The founders say 80 percent of users are under 35 and most join through referrals; campus testers, school leaderboards and an invite-only launch drove early spread ([source](https://www.foodnetwork.com/fn-dish/news/beli-app-trend)). The founders describe it as a solo tracking utility first, with social features growing later ([source](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/08/04/lifestyle/beli-restaurant-rating-app/)). A Columbia case study says growth is concentrated in large US cities ([source](https://caseworks.business.columbia.edu/caseworks/beli-heating-cold-start)). PitchBook lists a 46 million USD Series A in April 2026 ([source](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/509955-85)); I found no press report, so treat the amount as unverified.

What transferred. The ranking mechanic was copied to film by at least six apps in 2026 (see table). Reelist deliberately drops leaderboards and streaks, caps comparisons at ten, and allows ties, citing comparison fatigue and "apples to oranges" complaints ([source](https://reelist.watch/vs/beli)). The mechanic is easy to copy. Beli's distribution (campuses, city density, visible leaderboards) did not obviously transfer: none of the film clones shows scale.

## Mood and emotion recommenders

Products. Mood pickers ask how you want to feel and return titles: moveme.tv, Taranify (a colour quiz), Lumigo. Reviewers call them a "gimmick", a "consumer toy", and "more vibes than science", and note opaque methods and frequent misses ([source](https://declom.com/moveme.tv), [source](https://theaitoolbible.com/tools/taranify), [source](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46044752)). I found no retention or usage data for any of them (unverified).

Research. A scoping review of 83 papers on emotion-based film search and recommendation found a "large gap" between emotion science and the systems built, with ad hoc measures and little theory ([source](https://doi.org/10.1155/2022/7831013)). One controlled study (115 people) found that diversifying recommendations by emotions drawn from audience reviews improved satisfaction without hurting quality ([source](https://doi.org/10.1145/3743147)).

Large platforms. Netflix launched "AI Discover" on 3 November 2026 for some US TV users: pick a mood theme, get titles, tune the results. It is not a chatbot ([source](https://www.indiewire.com/news/general-news/netflix-ai-discover-function-1235225158/)). Hub's October 2026 survey says 41 percent of US viewers use AI tools to find shows, and 54 percent of those aged 16 to 34 ([source](https://www.tvtechnology.com/insights/analysis/hub-despite-ai-most-viewers-still-rely-on-word-of-mouth-to-find-new-tv-shows)).

Interpretation (mine, not sourced): the gimmick complaint targets guessing a mood before viewing. Moodie records a response after viewing. That is a different claim, but users may not see the difference from the name or the first screen.

## Design and positioning references

- **MUBI.** Every film is hand-picked and carries an "Our Take" explaining why ([source](https://help.mubi.com/article/22-what-makes-mubi-special)). Its 2012 model showed 30 films, adding one and removing one each day, which made a small catalogue feel like a strength ([source](https://www.vulture.com/article/mubi-art-house-movies-streamer-distributor.html)). Its identity uses colour to express each film's mood, and its messaging rules are: speak from the heart, add context, do not assume prior knowledge, tell stories about film ([source](https://gaggio.com/work/mubi/)). A caution from the same source: MUBI's own research found its look and language read as "niche, difficult", which was a barrier to growth.
- **Criterion Channel.** A stable library of more than 1,000 films plus rotating programmes and supplements, launched in 2019 ([source](https://www.wallshelfjournal.com/screen-and-story/curated-rotation-or-deep-archive-how-to-choose-between-mubi-and-the-criterion-ch)); this is a secondary source.
- **Letterboxd Video Store.** Framed as "employee picks" shelves chosen from member watchlists ([source](https://letterboxd.com/journal/letterboxd-video-store/)).

Common thread: a named human reason beside each title, limited choice, and editorial context.

## Platform and licensing constraints

**TMDB.** The developer licence "does not permit any commercial use". Charging for an app that uses TMDB content, or earning from recommending content with it, needs a separate written agreement that may carry fees ([source](https://www.themoviedb.org/api-terms-of-use)). TMDB staff said in October 2026: free use is fine with no revenue; an in-app purchase, ads or a subscription means upgrading to a commercial plan ([source](https://www.themoviedb.org/talk/6ac825fa562024b498444de6)). Commercial pricing is not public (unverified). Other terms: attribution with the TMDB logo and a not-endorsed notice; no caching beyond six months ([source](https://www.themoviedb.org/api-terms-of-use)), which staff say is met by refreshing periodically ([source](https://www.themoviedb.org/talk/6a15289d321e33029fc21e56)). The terms also bar use "in connection with, including for training" an ML or AI application, and list LLM query-response systems as commercial use ([source](https://www.themoviedb.org/api-terms-of-use)). Staff say the clause targets training LLMs on TMDB data and that semantic search is acceptable ([source](https://www.themoviedb.org/talk/6ac5215a88a7db873bc20783), [source](https://www.themoviedb.org/talk/6a15289d321e33029fc21e56)). Forum replies are not a contract.

**App Store.** Guideline 1.2 requires apps with user content to filter objectionable material, offer reporting with timely response, let users block abusers, and publish contact details ([source](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). In February 2026 Apple clarified that random or anonymous chat falls under 1.2 ([source](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=d75yllv4)). In October 2026 it added nonconsensual intimate imagery, including AI-generated, as content apps must prevent and remove ([source](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=vvzx6cul)). Age ratings now include 13+, 16+ and 18+, with a new required questionnaire ([source](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=ks775ehf)). State app store age laws in Texas, Utah, Louisiana and California add age-signal duties for developers from 2026, subject to court challenges ([source](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/app-store-accountability-acts/)).

## What nobody does well

1. **The reason survives until the viewing.** Letterboxd's watchlist cannot hold a note ([source](https://support.letterboxd.com/hc/en-us/articles/15179261056143-What-s-the-difference-between-my-lists-and-my-watchlist)); a user built a browser extension in 2026 to add one ([source](https://github.com/LeonGeorgi/letterboxd-watchlist-notes/blob/main/PRIVACY.md)). The small apps store who and sometimes why, but as a field on a saved item, not as something shown again at the moment of watching (not found in any listing).
2. **A real reply, not a receipt.** The best existing loop closures are a finish notification (LaterRex) and a thank-you (Somebody Once Told Me). LaterRex states "no chat" ([source](https://laterrex.com/blog/track-movies-tv-books-games-in-one-app)). Nobody found sends back what the film was like for the viewer, or makes disagreement a normal reply.
3. **Feeling as the record, not decoration.** Beli-style apps avoid typing a number but still produce a score. Plex's emoji reactions sit next to star ratings ([source](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/03/plex-launches-new-social-features-amid-a-major-price-hike/)). No product found uses a chosen mix of feelings plus a first-person note as the main response and as the basis for matching people.
4. **Taste paths with visible evidence.** Match scores and taste-match percentages exist ([Plex](https://www.plex.tv/blog/plex-introduces-a-social-platform-for-entertainment-discovery-across-streaming-services/), [Ranked](https://rankedmovies.app/guides/beli-for-movies/), [Reelist](https://reelist.watch/faq)). They are numbers. None found shows the shared film and the two people's words that justify the path.
5. **AI as a stated choice.** Products either build AI in (Likewise, Simkl, Netflix) or have none (Letterboxd, LaterRex). No product found asks at onboarding. Fable shows the cost of AI by default ([source](https://www.wired.com/story/fable-controversy-ai-summaries/)). Recalo already lets users connect ChatGPT or Claude ([source](https://recaloapp.com/features/)), so agent connection alone is not unique.
6. **Trust that the record will last.** TV Time deleted user data on closure, Musicboard had no export, Likewise users report no export ([source](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/02/popular-tv-tracking-app-tv-time-is-shutting-down-as-company-focuses-on-ai/), [source](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/09/so-whats-going-on-with-musicboard/), [source](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?hl=en_US&id=com.Likewise.apps.Likewise)). Export from day one and a plain statement of how the app pays for itself would be a visible difference.
7. **Curated feel inside a social tracker.** The curated references (MUBI, Criterion) are streaming services. The trackers look like databases. No tracker found pairs each title with a named person's reason as its main presentation.

## Risks and things that contradict the thesis

1. **Friend features have failed at very large scale.** Netflix had the audience and the catalogue and still reached only 2 to 8 percent ([source](https://www.mindtheproduct.com/why-we-fail-what-i-learned-from-5-years-with-friends-netflixs-social-strategy/)). Spotify removed messaging once for low use ([source](https://musically.com/2025/08/27/spotify-brings-messaging-back-eight-years-after-removing-it/)). The lesson both drew: most people will not build a friend graph inside a media app.
2. **Cold start.** Beli worked as a solo tool first and grew through dense local groups ([source](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/08/04/lifestyle/beli-restaurant-rating-app/), [source](https://www.foodnetwork.com/fn-dish/news/beli-app-trend)). A two-person recommendation is worthless until the second person joins. LaterRex's answer is that sends work for people without the app ([source](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/laterrex-movie-tv-backlog/id6757280650)).
3. **The niche is crowded and unproven.** Eight or more small apps target "who recommended it", and none shows traction. That can mean poor execution or weak demand; this research cannot tell which.
4. **Friends are not clearly the top discovery channel.** Ampere's 2025 global survey found algorithm picks (26 percent) ahead of word of mouth (23 percent) ([source](https://www.ampereanalysis.com/insight/streaming-algorithms-now-beat-word-of-mouth-for-tv-and-movie-picks)). YouGov finds 71 percent of US Gen Z discover shows through social media, above friends and family at 67 percent ([source](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/53847-how-americans-discover-new-tv-shows)). In support of the thesis, Hub found 29 percent of new favourite shows came from someone the viewer knows versus 14 percent from a streamer's recommendations ([source](https://www.tvtechnology.com/insights/analysis/hub-despite-ai-most-viewers-still-rely-on-word-of-mouth-to-find-new-tv-shows)). The data is mixed and is mostly about TV.
5. **Incumbents can add this.** Letterboxd has 30 million members, private friend sharing, a Close Friends mode, and may soon have a well-funded owner. Plex is shipping feeling reactions and match scores to 42 million users. Spotify brought messaging back in 2025 ([source](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2025-08-26/introducing-messages-a-new-way-to-share-what-you-love-on-spotify-with-friends-and-family/)). A send-with-reason feature is likely a small build for any of them (my judgment).
6. **Mood reads as a gimmick.** See above. The name "Moodie" points at the weakest part of the category.
7. **Free trackers struggle to survive.** TV Time found no paid demand at 25 million users. Trakt doubled prices and drew a user backlash ([source](https://alternativeto.net/news/2025/2/trakt-tv-has-set-stricter-limits-for-free-users-and-raised-vip-subscription-prices-by-100-/)). Serializd added ads because its founder would not charge for features ([source](https://www.patreon.com/Serializd/posts/rewatching-2025-147185394)). Likewise moved to newsletters.
8. **TMDB cost and AI terms.** Any revenue triggers a commercial agreement of unknown price. The written AI clause is broader than staff comments suggest. An AI-enabled mode, a learned recommender, or an MCP server that returns catalogue data all need a written answer from TMDB before launch.
9. **Moderation load.** Private replies, comments and optional photos bring the full 1.2 duties, plus image moderation, for a one-person team.
10. **Aesthetic risk.** A festival or museum look can signal "not for me", as MUBI found ([source](https://gaggio.com/work/mubi/)). The people who exchange recommendations most may be casual viewers. One Beli-for-film builder chose that audience on purpose because Letterboxd felt built for hobbyists ([source](https://rileyjameson.com/notes/building-popcorn/)).

## Open questions to validate with real users

1. When someone last watched a film a friend suggested, did they tell the friend? Through what channel? Would they have used an app for it?
2. Is the forgotten reason a felt problem, or only a nice idea? Ask people to show their current list and say who suggested each title.
3. Does choosing feelings after a film feel natural or like homework? How does completion compare with a single tap?
4. Do people trust a path through "someone who responded like you to a shared film" more than a match percentage? Does showing the evidence change that?
5. How many people must a new user know on the app before it is useful? Is one enough?
6. Does a recommendation sent to someone without the app get opened, and does it bring them in?
7. Does a visible "watched it, not for me" reply damage the relationship or the habit of recommending?
8. Where do target users' recommendations actually come from: friends, or creators and social feeds?
9. Would people choose the AI-disabled mode, and does offering the choice raise trust even among those who enable it?
10. Would anyone pay, and for what? This decides the TMDB licence question.
11. Does the premium look attract or exclude the casual friend pairs the product depends on?
12. Are displaced TV Time users a reachable first audience, given that film comes first and TV later?
