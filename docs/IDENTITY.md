# Identity

Written 2026-11-13. This answers four questions: what Moodie is, why someone would start, why they would stay, and what that means for what gets built. It follows [PHILOSOPHY.md](PHILOSOPHY.md) and draws on [MARKET_LANDSCAPE.md](MARKET_LANDSCAPE.md), whose claims are sourced there and have not been checked a second time. Opinions here are marked as such.

## What it is

Moodie is a film and television diary where a recommendation from a person stays whole: who sent it, why, what you made of it, and what you said back.

It is for people who already swap recommendations with friends and already lose track of them. It is not for people who want an algorithm to pick tonight's film.

## What the market says

- **Remembering who recommended something is no longer new.** At least eight small apps do some version of it. None shows scale.
- **Nobody connects the whole loop.** A recommendation with a reason, the later viewing, a response that is not a number, and a reply to the person who sent it. This is the open ground.
- **Mood pickers are read as gimmicks.** Reviewers of pick-a-mood products use that word. The complaint is about guessing a mood before watching. Recording a response after watching is a different act, but the name and first screen have to make that obvious.
- **Friend features fail when they are an extra.** Netflix removed its friends feature at about 2 percent use. Spotify removed messaging once. The lesson is not that people do not recommend to friends. It is that a friends tab bolted onto a catalogue goes unused.
- **Beli worked alone first.** Its founders describe a solo tracking tool that became social later, spread through dense local groups.
- **Trackers die and take the data with them.** TV Time closed in July 2026 with 25 million accounts. People now ask whether their record will last.
- **Incumbents are moving.** Plex shipped feeling reactions and a match score. Letterboxd has 30 million members. A send-with-a-note feature is small for them to add.

## Why someone starts

In order of how likely each is to bring a person in (my judgement):

1. **Someone sends them a film.** The invitation is the product. A recommendation must be readable, and answerable, by a person who has no account yet. Not built.
2. **Their diary is worth keeping alone.** A clean log with a note and a few words is useful on day one with no friends. Built.
3. **Their history comes with them.** Import from a Letterboxd export. Not built.

## Why someone stays

1. **The watchlist remembers.** Every title on it says who and why. Built.
2. **Their friend hears back.** Watching a recommended film leads to a reply, and disagreement is a normal reply. Built.
3. **They see the same film through another person.** Two responses to one film, side by side. Built.
4. **A quiet reminder brings a title back with its reason.** Not built.
5. **The record is theirs.** Export, and a plain statement of how Moodie pays for itself. Not built.

## What is true and different

- Every title on your watchlist carries a person and a reason.
- A response is words: what you felt, what stood out, a note. No stars, no score, no percentage, for films or for people.
- When two people have seen the same film you can read both responses. That, not a match number, is the evidence for following someone's taste.
- AI is a choice made on the first screen and enforced on the server.
- The interface never sells to the person using it.

## What Moodie is not

- Not a mood picker. It does not ask how you want to feel and prescribe a film. (The "choose a feeling" filter and the search-by-feeling tab were removed from the web app for this reason.)
- Not a ranking game. No leaderboards, streaks or badges.
- Not a catalogue. It does not try to show everything; it shows what people you know responded to.
- Not a chatbot.

## What this means for the order of work

Opinion, ranked by how much each strengthens starting or staying:

1. A recommendation link that works for someone without an account.
2. Reminders that carry the person and the reason.
3. Television.
4. Letterboxd import, and export.
5. A feeling vocabulary made for film (see [FEELINGS.md](FEELINGS.md)).
6. The profile as a shelf: favourites, what a person is recommending.

## The name

"Moodie" points at the part of the category people distrust. The product is about people and what they say to each other, and the name says mood.

This is your decision. If the name changes, it should change before the App Store listing, because the bundle identifier and listing name are slow to change later. Directions worth exploring, none checked for trademarks, domains or App Store availability: a name about passing something on (word of mouth, hand to hand), a name about the reply (how it landed, afterwards), or a plain invented word with no category meaning.
